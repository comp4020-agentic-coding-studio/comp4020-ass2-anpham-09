# Policies and support

## Oral history ethics guidelines

These apply from week 7 onward, and they are not optional. Your dig site was
made by people, some of whom are reachable, and a course assignment is not a
reason to treat them as a data source.

If you contact a former contributor to your dig site:

1. **Introduce yourself.** Name the course, the university, and your purpose.
2. **Ask permission.** Participation is voluntary. A non-response is a no.
3. **Explain how their words will be used.** Your dig report is a course
   assignment, not a publication, but treat their contribution with the same
   care.
4. **Respect withdrawal.** A participant can withdraw at any time, and you will
   remove their contribution from your report.
5. **Do not contact someone who has explicitly archived or deleted their
   project with a statement explaining why.** That statement is a boundary.

These guidelines apply to all contact: email, social media, issue trackers, and
in-person conversation.

If you are unsure whether an approach is appropriate, ask Kai before you send
anything. "I already sent it" is not a position anyone can help you from.

## Choosing and changing your dig site

Your dig site is chosen in week 1 and carried through to week 12. Changing it
after week 4 means repeating four weeks of survey work in your own time, so
choose carefully rather than quickly.

If your dig site turns out to be unworkable — the history was rebased flat, the
licence prohibits forking, or it was not as dead as it looked — talk to Maren in
week 2 or 3. A dig site that cannot support the semester is a legitimate reason
to switch, and an early switch costs you very little.

## Academic integrity

The analysis must be yours. You may use any tool you like to produce your
timelines, dependency reports, and visualisations — the graph is the artefact,
the tool is not — but the reading of the evidence, the diagnosis, and the
ethical position are what is marked, and they must be your own work.

Cite your dig site's commits, files, and documentation the way you would cite
any primary source. If you quote a former maintainer, quote them accurately and
with their permission.

If you use a generative tool to help draft or structure your writing, say so and
say where. Declaring it costs you nothing. Not declaring it is misconduct.

## Late work and extensions

Assessments are due at 12:00 on the date listed on each brief. Late submissions
without an approved extension lose 5% of the available marks per day.

Extensions are granted for the reasons the university recognises — illness,
carer responsibilities, and circumstances outside your control. Ask before the
deadline where you can. An unreachable maintainer or a dig site that will not
build is not grounds for an extension; both are findings, and both belong in
your report.

## Getting help

Bring dig-site problems to the weekly session. Most of them are more
interesting with an audience, and the class will have seen the same failure
modes on their own sites.

For anything that cannot wait, Maren handles course-level questions and Kai
handles dig-site advice and oral-history ethics. Contact details are on the
[People](/people/) page.
