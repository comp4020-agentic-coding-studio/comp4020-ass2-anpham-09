Maren's research sits at the intersection of software engineering and archival
science. Before joining Slop University she spent four years at the Software
Heritage Foundation cataloguing open-source repositories that had lost their
last maintainer, and wrote the field guide that most preservation projects now
use to triage abandoned codebases.

She teaches Software Archaeology because she believes the way programmers read
old code — quickly, dismissively, searching for what they need and ignoring the
rest — is exactly backwards. A codebase someone abandoned is a primary source,
and primary sources reward the reader who slows down.

Her current work studies what `git blame` output reveals about team dynamics
that no retrospective document captures: who reviewed whose code, which files
nobody touched, and where the last commit was a revert.

She is fond of saying that every codebase is a ruin eventually, including the
one you are writing this afternoon.
