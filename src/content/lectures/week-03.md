## Learning outcomes

By the end of this week you should be able to:

1. classify a corpus of commit messages and infer communication norms from the distribution
2. read version-control trailers as provenance metadata
3. identify what the documentary record structurally cannot contain

## Outline

- **The commit message as a genre:** conventions (Conventional Commits, Angular, kernel style) and what choosing one says about a team
- **The information hierarchy:** subject line, body, trailers, co-authors
- **What terse messages mean:** velocity, solo work, exhaustion, or indifference?
- **Case study: the Linux kernel** — Torvalds's merge-commit messages as editorial commentary on other people's code
- **The `Signed-off-by` trailer and the Developer Certificate of Origin:** provenance tracking as archaeological metadata
- **What commit messages cannot tell you:** the conversation that happened before the code was written

## Seminar questions

1. Bodel warns that surviving inscriptions over-represent those with the resources to commission them. What is the equivalent survivorship bias in a commit log?
2. A team adopts Conventional Commits in month fourteen. What does the adoption date tell you that the convention itself does not?
3. Can a commit message be evidence of morale, or only of convention? What would distinguish the two?
