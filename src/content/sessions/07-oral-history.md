## Before the dig

Investigate who contributed to your dig site:

1. `git shortlog -sn` gives you the contributor list ranked by commit count
2. GitHub profiles, personal websites, blog posts, conference talks — what can
   you learn about the top contributors without contacting them?
3. Are any of them still active elsewhere? Have they written about the project?

If you plan to contact a former maintainer, draft your message before the
session. The course's oral-history ethics guidelines apply: introduce yourself,
explain the course, ask permission, and make clear that participation is
voluntary and they can withdraw at any time.

## In the session

Discuss approach strategies as a class. What do you say to a stranger whose
abandoned project you have been studying for six weeks? What questions are
productive and which are intrusive? What if they archived the repo on purpose
and do not want it discussed?

Role-play an interview with a classmate playing the maintainer.

## Afterwards

If you contacted someone, write up what you learned. If you did not — they were
unreachable, or you decided it would be inappropriate — write up what you found
from secondary sources: blog posts, talks, mailing list archives, and what
questions remain unanswered.
