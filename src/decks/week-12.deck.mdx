# The exhibit

SLOP2374 · Week 12

---

## The museum problem

Your audience has never seen your dig site and never will.

A museum solves this every day: narrative first, artefacts in support,
interpretation offered, judgement invited.

---

## Structure

1. **The site** — what it was, who built it, when
2. **The excavation** — your findings
3. **The diagnosis** — cause of death, with evidence
4. **The ethics** — should it come back?
5. **The restoration** — what you brought back, and what you left

---

## Why that order

The audience has to care about the project before they can care about your
analysis of it.

Lead with method and you have written a report. Lead with the thing and you
have made an exhibit.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Specificity beats comprehensiveness.
>
> One well-told story is worth more than twelve findings listed.

---

## The commonest failure

Presenting all twelve weeks, evenly, in order.

You did twelve weeks of work. You are showing the three findings that earned
the conclusion. The rest is in the written companion.

---

## The written companion

2000 words that **complement** the presentation rather than transcribe it.

Put the detail here: the full stratigraphy, the complete restoration log, the
evidence you cited in passing.

---

## What you will read differently

You have spent a semester reading one codebase more carefully than its authors
ever did.

That is now a thing you know how to do — including on code you wrote, which
will one day be somebody else's dig site.
