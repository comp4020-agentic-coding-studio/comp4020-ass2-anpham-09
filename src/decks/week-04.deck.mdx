# The tool kit

SLOP8374 · Week 4

---

## A manifest is a time capsule

Pottery dates a stratum. `package.json` dates a project.

You can usually place a codebase within about eighteen months from its direct
dependencies alone, without reading a line of its code.

---

## Era markers

- `request` → before 2020
- jQuery 1.x → before 2016
- `moment` → before the Temporal conversation
- Bower, Grunt, `node-sass` → each names a year

Not every marker is a judgement. All of them are a date.

---

## Pinning as a worldview

Exact pins say: change is a risk to be prevented.

Loose ranges say: change is a benefit to be received.

Neither is wrong. Both tell you what the team was afraid of.

---

## The lockfile as snapshot

`package-lock.json`, `yarn.lock`, `Gemfile.lock`, `poetry.lock`.

A lockfile records the exact versions resolved at one moment — and says, in
effect: this worked on my machine, on this day, and nowhere else.

An abandoned project with a lockfile hands you a reproducibility target. The
target may no longer exist.

---

## Transitive dependencies

Your direct dependencies have dependencies. Those have dependencies.

A manifest listing twelve packages can resolve to several hundred.

You chose twelve. You inherited the rest, written by strangers. The question
for a dig site is how many of those strangers were still maintaining their
code on the day this project stopped.

---

## Case study: left-pad

In 2016 an eleven-line package was unpublished from npm.

Thousands of builds broke, including Babel's and React's.

The lesson was not about one developer. It was that nobody knew what was in
their tool kit — the transitive graph is the part you did not choose and cannot
run without.

---

## When the tool kit is dead too

Check every dependency's own last release.

A project abandoned in 2021 that depended on something abandoned in 2019 was
already being maintained on borrowed time.

Abandonment is often inherited.

---

## Security archaeology

Known vulnerabilities in abandoned dependencies do not expire.

They sit in the ground, fully armed, waiting for someone to run `npm install`
on a ten-year-old lockfile.
