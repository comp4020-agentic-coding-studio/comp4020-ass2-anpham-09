# Finding the people

SLOP8374 · Week 7

---

## The cast list

`git shortlog -sn` ranks contributors by commit count.

It is a cast list, not a credit roll: it omits reviewers, issue triagers,
documentation writers, and whoever paid for any of it.

---

## Trails

People leave a digital presence: profiles, blogs, talks, mailing lists,
conference videos.

Most of what you need is already public. Start there, and go to a person only
when the artefacts run out.

---

## Borrowed, not invented

We are not inventing a method here. We are borrowing one.

| Field | Source type | Governing principle |
|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| History | Veterans, survivors | The interviewee owns their story |
| Anthropology | Community members | Do no harm to your subjects |
| Journalism | Sources, witnesses | On the record versus off it |
| Software | Former maintainers | All of the above |

The Oral History Association, founded in 1966, has been arguing about these
rules for longer than most of our dig sites existed.

---

## Ethics first

1. Introduce yourself
2. Ask permission
3. Explain how their words will be used
4. Respect withdrawal
5. Do not contact someone who archived their project with a statement

A non-response is a no.

## Case study: Roads and Bridges

Nadia Eghbal's 2016 report documented the invisible, unpaid labour holding up
critical open-source infrastructure.

The maintainers in it were not famous. They were load-bearing — which is not the
same thing, and is much harder to see from a commit graph.

---

## What living sources know

- The argument that led to the rewrite
- The sponsor who quietly withdrew
- The burnout nobody posted about
- Why the obvious fix was never applied

None of this is in any artefact. That is the whole reason to ask.

---

## When contact is not possible

Unreachable, uninterested, or inappropriate to approach.

Then say so. Document the secondary sources you used, and name the questions
that stay open.

An acknowledged gap is a finding. A guess dressed as a finding is not.
